## hook
In 862{eight sixty-two}, a group of tribes in the north looked around and said:
"Our land is great and rich, but there is no order in it. Come and rule over us."
They said this to Vikings.

Eleven centuries later, that land is the biggest country on Earth.
It has eleven time zones, and it has been ruled by Vikings, Mongols, tsars and communists.
This is the entire history of Russia. Let's go.

## rus
The Viking was called Rurik, and the town was Novgorod.
His successor, Oleg, sailed south and took a city called Kyiv.
So the first Russian state, Kievan Rus, was run from what is now Ukraine.

It got rich by rowing furs, honey and slaves down the rivers to Constantinople,
which the Vikings simply called "the Great City".

## faith
Around 986{nine eighty-six}, Prince Vladimir decided his people needed one religion, so he went shopping.
Islam looked good, until he heard it banned alcohol.
According to the chronicle, he said: "Drinking is the joy of the Rus. We cannot live without it."
Then his envoys saw the great church in Constantinople, and said they didn't know if they were in heaven or on Earth.

So in 988{nine eighty-eight}, Vladimir chose Orthodox Christianity, and baptized the whole of Kyiv in the river.

## mongols
After Vladimir, his descendants split the land into small principalities, and spent the next two centuries fighting each other.
That was a mistake, because in 1237{twelve thirty-seven}, the Mongols arrived.
Batu Khan, a grandson of Genghis Khan, burned city after city, and in 1240{twelve forty} he destroyed Kyiv.

For the next two hundred and forty years, Russian princes paid tribute to the Golden Horde.
To rule their own cities, they had to travel to the Khan and ask for a permission slip.

## moscow
And this is where a small wooden town called Moscow had a brilliant idea: become the Mongols' best tax collector.
One prince was nicknamed Ivan Moneybag. Moscow grew rich, then strong.

In 1480{fourteen eighty}, Ivan the Third stopped paying.
The Mongol army came to the Ugra river, and the two armies stared at each other for weeks.
Then both went home.
And that's how the Mongol yoke ended: with the most important battle in Russian history, where nobody fought.

Ivan married the niece of the last Byzantine emperor, took the double-headed eagle, and Moscow began calling itself the Third Rome.

## terrible
His grandson, Ivan the Fourth, was crowned in 1547{fifteen forty-seven} as the first Tsar, from the word Caesar.
He conquered Kazan and Astrakhan, and built Saint Basil's Cathedral to celebrate.
Then his beloved wife died, and Ivan became Ivan the Terrible.

His secret police rode in black, with dog heads and brooms on their saddles, to sniff out traitors and sweep them away.
In a rage, he struck his own son and heir with his staff, and killed him.
Ivan died a few years later, while setting up a game of chess.

## siberia
Meanwhile, a rich merchant family hired a Cossack named Yermak and a few hundred men to cross the Urals.
In 1582{fifteen eighty-two} they took the capital of the Khanate of Sibir.
After that, Russians chased furs east, all the way to the Pacific Ocean, in under sixty years.

## troubles
Ivan's surviving son died without an heir, and more than seven hundred years of Rurik's dynasty were over.
What followed is literally called the Time of Troubles.
First a terrible famine. Then a man appeared claiming to be Ivan's youngest son, who had died years earlier.
With Polish help, this False Dmitry actually became tsar. Within a year, he was killed.
Legend says his ashes were fired from a cannon, back towards Poland.
Then a second False Dmitry appeared. And then the Poles took Moscow.

In 1612{sixteen twelve}, a butcher and a prince raised an army and threw them out.
And in 1613{sixteen thirteen}, Russia elected a sixteen-year-old named Michael Romanov.
His family would rule for three hundred and four years.

## peter
His grandson, Peter, was two meters tall, and he had seen the future: it was in Holland.
He toured Western Europe in disguise as a regular guy named Peter Mikhailov, which fooled nobody, because he was two meters tall.
He worked in a Dutch shipyard, came home, and personally cut off his nobles' beards.
Anyone who wanted to keep one had to pay a beard tax.

Then he fought Sweden for twenty-one years. He lost the first big battle, rebuilt his army, and crushed the Swedes at Poltava.
On a swamp he took from them, he built a brand new capital, Saint Petersburg.
In 1721{seventeen twenty-one}, he declared himself Emperor. Russia was now an empire.
Peter also had his own son arrested and tortured, and the young man died in prison.
That is the second ruler in this video to kill his own son. Keep count.

## catherine
Fast forward to 1762{seventeen sixty-two}. Emperor Peter the Third was obsessed with Prussia, the country Russia was fighting, so he simply made peace and gave back what Russia had won.
His wife, a German princess, had other plans.
Six months in, she and the palace guards overthrew him.
A week later he was dead, officially of haemorrhoidal colic.

She became Catherine the Great.
She took Crimea, and with Prussia and Austria, she carved up Poland until Poland disappeared from the map.
Then a Cossack named Pugachev started a huge rebellion by claiming to be her dead husband.
Because in Russia, pretending to be a dead royal was basically a career.

## napoleon
In 1812{eighteen twelve}, Napoleon invaded with about six hundred thousand men, the biggest army Europe had ever seen.
The Russians kept retreating, and burned everything behind them.
At Borodino, about seventy thousand men were killed or wounded in a single day.
Napoleon took Moscow, and it was empty. Then Moscow burned.
He waited five weeks for the Tsar to surrender. The letter never came.
So he marched home, in the Russian winter.
Only a fraction of his army made it out. Two years later, Russian Cossacks were camping in the middle of Paris.

## reform
But most Russians were still serfs: farmers who belonged to a landlord, and could be sold.
After a humiliating defeat in the Crimean War, Tsar Alexander the Second freed twenty-three million serfs in 1861{eighteen sixty-one}.
Six years later, he sold Alaska to the United States for seven point two million dollars, about two cents an acre.
Americans mocked it as "Seward's Folly". Then they found gold. And oil.
Oops.

Alexander survived several assassination attempts. In 1881{eighteen eighty-one}, a bomb finally killed him.

## nicholas
The last Tsar, Nicholas the Second, blundered into a war with Japan in 1904{nineteen oh four}.
He sent his Baltic fleet halfway around the world.
On the way, in the North Sea, it opened fire on British fishing boats, because it thought they were Japanese.
When the fleet finally reached Japan, it was destroyed in about a day.
Back home, soldiers fired on peaceful marchers, and a revolution forced the Tsar to accept a parliament.

Then came World War One, and millions of Russian casualties.
At court, a Siberian mystic named Rasputin seemed to help the Tsar's sick son, and gained huge influence.
Nobles murdered him in 1916{nineteen sixteen}.
Legend says he survived poison and bullets before they threw him in a frozen river. The autopsy says it was mostly the bullets.

## revolution
In February 1917{nineteen seventeen}, bread riots broke out, the soldiers joined them, and Nicholas gave up the throne.
Three hundred and four years of Romanovs, gone in a week.
Then Germany shipped a revolutionary named Lenin home from Switzerland in a sealed train, hoping he would cause chaos.
He did.
His Bolsheviks seized power in the October Revolution, which happened in November, because Russia was still using an older calendar.

A brutal civil war followed, Reds against Whites.
In 1918{nineteen eighteen}, the Tsar and his whole family were shot in a basement.
The Reds won, and in 1922{nineteen twenty-two}, they created the Soviet Union.
Lenin died two years later. His body is still on display in Moscow.

## stalin
The next leader called himself Stalin, the man of steel.
He forced farmers into giant collective farms, and the famine that followed killed millions, especially in Ukraine.
In the Great Terror, about seven hundred thousand people were shot in two years, and millions more were sent to labor camps called the Gulag.
Some victims were even edited out of photographs.

In 1939{nineteen thirty-nine}, Stalin made a deal with Hitler. Two years later, Hitler broke it, and invaded with millions of soldiers.
Leningrad was under siege for almost nine hundred days.
At Stalingrad, the Red Army finally turned the tide.
The Soviet Union lost about twenty-seven million people, more than any other country in the war.
In May 1945{nineteen forty-five}, the Red flag flew over Berlin.

## coldwar
Now there were two superpowers, and they hated each other.
In 1957{nineteen fifty-seven}, the Soviets launched Sputnik, the first satellite. America panicked.
Then a dog named Laika.
And in 1961{nineteen sixty-one}, Yuri Gagarin became the first human in space.
As the rocket lifted off, he said: "Poyekhali!" "Let's go!"

A year later, Soviet missiles in Cuba brought the world closer to nuclear war than ever before.
Then came long years of stagnation, empty shelves, a ten-year war in Afghanistan, and in 1986{nineteen eighty-six}, the disaster at Chernobyl.

## collapse
The new leader, Mikhail Gorbachev, tried to fix the system with openness and reform.
But once people could speak freely, they said they wanted out.
In 1989{nineteen eighty-nine}, the Berlin Wall fell.
In 1991{nineteen ninety-one}, hardliners tried a coup, and Boris Yeltsin climbed onto a tank to stop them. It failed in three days.
On Christmas Day, Gorbachev resigned, and the Soviet Union split into fifteen countries.
Six years later, Gorbachev starred in a Pizza Hut commercial.

## modern
The nineteen-nineties were chaos. Prices exploded, savings vanished, and a few men bought entire industries for almost nothing.
On New Year's Eve 1999{nineteen ninety-nine}, Yeltsin resigned on live TV, and handed power to a former KGB officer, Vladimir Putin.
Putin has led Russia ever since, as president or prime minister.
In 2014{twenty fourteen}, Russia annexed Crimea.
And in 2022{twenty twenty-two}, it launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine, the biggest war in Europe since 1945{nineteen forty-five}.
